Frozen shoulder runs through three stages: increasing pain, then freezing with loss of movement, then thawing and gradual recovery. Knowing which stage you are in determines the whole shape of treatment.

In the painful stage we do not push the joint; we focus on easing pain and preserving what range remains. In the frozen stage, graded mobilisation becomes the main treatment.

This condition demands patience: progress is measured in weeks and months, not in sessions. Sticking to the home programme shortens that considerably, while neglecting it lengthens it and can leave permanent restriction.

People with diabetes are more prone to it and take longer to recover — that is expected and does not mean treatment has failed.
